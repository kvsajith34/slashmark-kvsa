The Amazon rainforest is one of Earth's most biodiverse ecosystems, spanning
over 5.5 million square kilometers across nine countries in South America.
It is often called the "lungs of the planet" because of the enormous amount
of carbon dioxide it absorbs and the oxygen it produces.

Deforestation, driven primarily by agricultural expansion, logging, and
infrastructure development, poses the greatest threat to the Amazon. Each
year, thousands of square kilometers of forest are cleared, releasing stored
carbon into the atmosphere and destroying habitats for countless species.

Indigenous communities have lived in harmony with the forest for thousands
of years. Their traditional knowledge of medicinal plants and sustainable
land management is invaluable and increasingly recognized by scientists.

Conservation efforts include establishing protected areas, supporting
sustainable agriculture practices, and strengthening indigenous land rights.
International cooperation and funding are critical to making meaningful
progress in protecting what remains of this irreplaceable ecosystem.

Climate change further threatens the Amazon through rising temperatures and
altered rainfall patterns, which can push parts of the forest toward a
tipping point where it becomes a savannah rather than a rainforest. Urgent
action is needed to prevent this catastrophic transformation.
